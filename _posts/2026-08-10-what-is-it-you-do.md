My name is Richard and I am an AI survivor and technical writer. I use _honey_ conversationally, despite the only Southern part of me being from California. And one of life's questions that I've always found hardest to answer has been: 

### What do you do?

I started out in marketing where I did snake oil blogging (SEO), and for a while I was tech support where I stumbled into my destined profession: technical writing. 

Dear god I actually enjoy this crap. 

Technical writing is something that came naturally to me. I have no formal training, I just learned it and practiced it and now I'm confident and battle-tested.

And I can give a simple answer to what I do:


### I write instruction manuals.

Or so it went until my 80+ year old grandfather in law asked me another question: can't AI do that?

I am in the middle of the second year of AI transformation now. That question is like firecrackers to a dog. And it has forced another even more uncomfortable question: Am I still writing?

These days, I spend maybe 10-20% of my day writing and the rest is spent architecting, navigating, and ideating (atrocious word). 

Some technical writers reading this don't use AI as much as I do, others use it more (which is insane, honey, who hurt you?) and wherever you are in this spectrum, I think it's worth checking in on your answer to this question.

I think one of the biggest shifts professionally for me is how much of my job now involves communicating, very loudly (for me, anyway) what it is that I do here. In a sense, I'm still writing but that writing has shifted over to reports showing value rather than the end product of my profession. That goes to AI. I get the interesting stuff to do.

### I still tell people I'm a technical writer

I still follow it up with:

> I write the manual. 

because I think it's relatable. It still thrills me when someone goes:

> OMG, I guess someone does actually have to write those.

![Grinch Heart]({{ '/assets/images/images.png' | relative_url }})

But deep down in my Grinch heart, I feel that what I do is no longer so simple. I'm lamenting what AI took from this profession that I actually enjoyed.

Because dammit, there was a time where clocking in to solve these problems filled me with joy.

And now I am filled with 10x that joy.
