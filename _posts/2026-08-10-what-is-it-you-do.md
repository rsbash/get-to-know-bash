My name is Richard and I am an AI survivor and technical writer. I use _honey_ conversationally, despite the only Southern part of me being from California. And one of life's questions that I've always found hardest to answer has been: 

### What do you do?

I started out in marketing where I did snake oil blogging (SEO), and for a while I was tech support where I stumbled into my destined profession: technical writing. Dear god I actually enjoy this crap. Technical writing is something that came naturally to me. I have no formal training, I just learned it, practiced it, and now I'm just more confident and battle-tested. 

I can give a simple answer to what I do: 

### I write instruction manuals.

Or so it went until my 80+ year old grandfather in law asked me another question: can't AI do that?

I am in the middle of the second year of AI transformation now. That question is like firecrackers to a dog. And it has forced another even more uncomfortable question: Am I _still_ writing?

These days, I spend maybe 10-20% of my day _writing_ and the rest is spent architecting, navigating, and ideating (disgusting word). Sometimes ideating happens after work when I'm walking my dogs or out and about. 

Some technical writers reading this don't use AI as much as I do, others use it more (which is insane, honey, who hurt you?) and wherever you are in this spectrum, I think it's worth asking yourself this question. 

I think one of the biggest shifts professionally for me is how much of my job now involves communicating, very loudly (for me, anyway) what it is that I do here. In a sense, I'm still writing but that writing has shifted over to reports showing _value_ rather than the end product of my profession. That goes to AI. I get the _interesting_ stuff to do. 

### I still tell people I'm a technical writer

I still follow it up with:

> I write the manual. 

because I think it's relatable. It still thrills me when someone goes:

> OMG, I guess someone does actually have to write those.

![Grinch Heart](../assets/images/images.png)

But deep down in my Grinch heart, I feel that what I do is no longer so simple. I'll keep going because I have to, but I'm also lamenting what AI took from me about this profession that I actually enjoyed. 

Because dammit, there was a time where clocking in to solve these problems filled me with joy. 

And now I am filled with 10x that joy.
