## How to Change the Front & Rear Tires on a **DRT 1.1 Mountain Bike**  
*(Works for both tube‑type and tubeless setups – the steps are the same up to the point you decide whether you’ll be fitting a tube or sealing a tubeless rim.)*  

---

### 1️⃣  What You’ll Need  

| Item | Why It’s Needed |
|------|-----------------|
| **Bike stand** (or a sturdy upside‑down chair) | Keeps the bike stable and frees your hands |
| **Set of 5 mm & 6 mm Allen wrenches** (or a 5 mm “hex” key) | Most DRT 1.1 bolts are metric |
| **Tire levers** (2–3 plastic ones) | To pry the tire bead off the rim without damaging it |
| **Floor pump** with a good gauge (or a CO₂ inflator) | To bring the tire up to the correct pressure |
| **New tire** (same size as the old one – e.g., 27.5 × 2.2 in) | Replacement |
| **Tube** (if you’re using tube‑type) **or** sealant & tubeless valve (if you’re going tubeless) | Air‑holding component |
| **Cleaning rag** | Wipe rim and tire beads |
| **Optional:** rim strip (for tube‑type), tire boot (if the rim is damaged) | Safety extras |

> **Quick tip:** Keep the manufacturer’s recommended pressure on the sidewall of the tire (e.g., 30–40 psi) handy.  

---

### 2️⃣  Safety First  

1. **Shift the chain onto the smallest chainring** and **release the rear brake** (if you have rim brakes) so the wheel can spin freely.  
2. **Turn off any electronic shifting** (if your bike has it) to avoid accidental gear changes.  
3. **Wear gloves** if you have rough hands; they protect both you and the rim from scratches.  

---

### 3️⃣  General Workflow (Front & Rear – Same Steps)  

| Step | Action | Details |
|------|--------|---------|
| **A** | **Remove the wheel** | – **Front wheel:** Loosen the quick‑release (or thru‑axle) skewer, open the lever, and drop the wheel. <br>– **Rear wheel:** Shift the chain to the smallest cog, open the quick‑release or loosen the thru‑axle, then swing the wheel out. |
| **B** | **Deflate the tire completely** | Press the valve core (for Presta, unscrew the tiny top nut; for Schrader, press the pin). |
| **C** | **Break the bead** | Insert a tire lever under one side of the bead, pry the bead over the rim edge, then slide the lever around the rim until a good length is free. Use a second lever if needed. |
| **D** | **Remove the tire** | Pull the second bead off the opposite side and lift the tire off the rim. Set the tire aside. |
| **E** | **Inspect the rim** | Check for dents, sharp spokes, or worn rim tape. Replace a damaged rim strip or apply a boot if needed. |
| **F** | **Install the new tire (or the same tire if you’re just replacing the tube)** | - **First bead:** Start at the valve hole. Fit the bead into the rim and work around, pushing the tire onto the rim with your hands (not levers) until the last few centimeters are left. <br>- **Second bead:** Start opposite the valve and use tire levers **only** for the final 2–3 cm. Avoid snapping the lever into the rim; keep the lever’s curved edge against the tire, not the rim. |
| **G** | **Fit the tube (if applicable)** | 1️⃣ Lightly inflate the tube just enough so it keeps its shape. <br>2️⃣ Insert the valve through the rim hole, then tuck the tube into the tire, ensuring it isn’t twisted or pinched. |
| **H** | **Seat the second bead** | Push the tire fully onto the rim. The bead should “pop” into the rim channel. Run your thumb around the tire to feel for any uneven sections. |
| **I** | **Inflate** | 1️⃣ Inflate slowly, watching the bead snap into place. <br>2️⃣ Stop at the recommended pressure (30–40 psi for typical 27.5 × 2.2 in MTB tires). <br>3️⃣ For tubeless, add sealant (30–40 ml) and shake the wheel to distribute it. |
| **J** | **Re‑mount the wheel** | – **Front:** Slide the axle back into the dropouts, close the quick‑release lever (or tighten the thru‑axle) until it’s snug but not forced. <br>– **Rear:** Align the chain with the cassette, push the wheel into the dropout, and tighten the quick‑release/thru‑axle. Re‑engage the rear brake. |
| **K** | **Final check** | Spin the wheel; it should run true without wobble. Squeeze the tire to feel for a uniform “springy” feel. Double‑check that the brake pads clear the rim (or rotor) correctly. |

---

### 4️⃣  Front‑Wheel Specific Nuances  

| Issue | What to Do |
|-------|------------|
| **Disc‑brake rotor** (if present) | Loosen the rotor bolts just enough so the wheel can drop out; do **not** fully remove the rotor unless you’re also changing it. |
| **Suspended fork** | No extra steps – the fork’s axle system is the same as a quick‑release; just be sure the lockout (if any) is disengaged. |
| **Throttle-mounted fender** | Remove it if it blocks wheel removal; reinstall after the tire is seated. |

---

### 5️⃣  Rear‑Wheel Specific Nuances  

| Issue | What to Do |
|-------|------------|
| **Derailleur & cassette** | Make sure the **chain is on the smallest sprocket** before removal. This eases wheel removal and prevents the derailleur from catching on the axle. |
| **Rear disc‑brake rotor** | Same as front – loosen the rotor bolts enough to drop the wheel. Do **not** over‑tighten the rotor after re‑install; torque spec is usually **5 Nm** (check your bike’s manual). |
| **Freehub smoothness** | While the wheel is off, spin the cassette by hand; it should feel smooth with no grinding. If you hear noise, it may need a clean or service. |

---

### 6️⃣  Tube‑Type vs. Tubeless – Quick Decision Guide  

| Feature | Tube‑type | Tubeless |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| **Weight** | Slightly heavier (tube + valve) | Lighter |
| **Puncture handling** | Patch or replace tube | Plug and seal; usually can ride through small cuts |
| **Installation difficulty** | Easier for beginners | Requires airtight rim strip, valve, and sealant; a bit more prep |
| **Performance** | Good, but higher rolling resistance | Lower resistance, smoother ride |

> **If you’re converting a tube‑type DRT 1.1 to tubeless**, you’ll need: a tubeless‑ready rim (most modern DRTs are), a tubeless valve, rim tape (or use the rim’s built‑in strip), and sealant. Follow the same **A‑K** steps, skipping the tube insertion, and add sealant after the tire is fully seated.

---

### 7️⃣  Trouble‑Shooting Tips  

| Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| **Bead refuses to pop over the rim** | Tire is too stiff, rim is dirty, or bead is not aligned with the valve. | Clean the rim edge, warm the tire with a hair‑dryer for a minute, and try again. |
| **Pinched tube (“snake bite”)** | Tube caught between bead and rim while installing. | Deflate, reposition the tube, ensure it lies flat without folds, reseat the bead. |
| **Air leaks at valve** | Valve core not tightened, or valve not seated properly. | Tighten the Presta core (use a small wrench) or replace the valve if damaged. |
| **Sidewall bulge after inflating** | Tire not seated evenly; bead may be caught on a spoke nipple. | Deflate, massage the tire around the rim to free the bead, then reinflate. |
| **Disc‑brake rubbing after wheel install** | Wheel not fully seated, or carbon rotor mis‑aligned. | Loosen the through‑axle a bit, spin wheel, then retighten firmly. Ensure rotor sits flush in the caliper. |

## TL;DR – One‑Paragraph Summary  

1. Secure the DRT 1.1 on a stand, shift the rear to the smallest cog, and release the quick‑release (or thru‑axle).  
2. Deflate the tire, pry one bead off with tire levers, then remove the tire entirely.  
3. Inspect the rim, replace tape if needed, then mount the new tire’s first bead.  
4. Insert a lightly‑inflated tube (or a tubeless valve + sealant), seat the second bead, and fully inflate to the tyre‑wall’s recommended psi.  
5. Re‑mount the wheel, tighten the axle, re‑engage the brake, spin‑check for true, and you’re ready to ride.

---

**Happy riding!** If anything feels stuck or you hear any unusual noises after re‑assembly, give the wheel another spin and double‑check the axle torque and brake alignment before hitting the trail. 🚵‍♂️
